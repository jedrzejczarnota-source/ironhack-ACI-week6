# Future GTM sprints

These are the three post-MVP sprints from the attached **Game Studio Competitor Digest Presentation.pdf**. They describe validation and commercialization after the workflow MVP.

## Sprint 1 — Validate (2 weeks)

- **Goal:** Validate that the competitor snapshot is useful enough to trigger a follow-up conversation.
- **Target user/buyer:** 10 Product Managers, Marketing Managers, or Product Marketing Managers in Berlin, Warsaw, and Helsinki.
- **Channel or motion:** LinkedIn direct messages, Discord, `r/gamedev`, a conference touchpoint, and the website. The report would be based on a general strategy document, tailored to the genre in which a potential client operates.
- **Key deliverable:** One free rival snapshot plus a 15-minute feedback call for each participant.
- **Success metric:** At least 6 participants say the snapshot is useful, and at least 3 ask for another snapshot.

## Sprint 2 — Weekly Watch (2 weeks)

- **Goal:** Test recurring usage and whether a regular competitor watch creates enough value to support payment.
- **Target user/buyer:** Six studios, prioritizing interested studios carried over from Sprint 1.
- **Channel or motion:** Direct outreach to the interested studios. 
- **Key deliverable:** Credits that allow each studio to generate three reports over two weeks. Studios upload their own high-level strategy documents to the report generator (which also shows their commitment to the product).
- **Success metric:** At least 50% use all credits, email open rate exceeds 60%, and at least one studio makes a decision to pay.

## Sprint 3 — First Buyers (4 weeks)

- **Goal:** Convert validated demand into the first paid pilots.
- **Target user/buyer:** Pilot studios and small publishers.
- **Channel or motion:** Sales calls supported by a LinkedIn post.
- **Key deliverable:** A €99/month plan containing five reports.
- **Success metric:** Two paid buyers or three letters of intent, with a cost below €0.50 per report (calculated from the API usage).



## GTM learning loop

The sequence deliberately moves from qualitative usefulness, to repeat usage, to paid conversion. Each sprint reuses the same MVP output while tightening the audience and the commercial commitment.
